# Request: what this study needs to be published in three journals

You are an experienced reviewer and editor in machine learning and AI safety. This folder holds the plan of a study that has not been run yet:

- `STUDY_SUMMARY.md`: a three-page summary (start here).
- `STUDY_PROTOCOL_v2.3.md`: the full protocol (about 2,300 lines; read the sections you need).
- `REGISTRATION_v2.3.md`: the summary that will be preregistered on OSF.

No results exist. The team is one graduate student (the principal investigator), one independent annotator who will be a co-author, and possibly a faculty advisor. The cash budget for API calls is capped at US$128. The authors want to publish quickly, while the models studied are still current.

The authors are considering three journals:

1. **Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR)**
2. **Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research (JAIR)**
3. **Computers & Security** (Elsevier)

## What to do

For each journal:

1. **Fit.** How well does this study fit the journal's scope, audience and acceptance criteria, as you know them? Say what you are unsure of.
2. **Adjustments.** What would have to change for a good chance of acceptance: framing, research question, design, scope of models, related work, analysis, reporting, length and format, ethics statement, release of data and code. Split them into **must** (likely rejection without it) and **should** (strengthens the paper).
3. **Risks.** The two or three most likely reasons for rejection.

Then:

4. **Ranking.** Which of the three is the best first target, and the order after it. If another journal or venue is clearly a better fit than all three, name it and say why, in two or three sentences.
5. **Design changes.** The core design will be preregistered before any data exist and should not change after that. Mark every adjustment that would change the registered design, so the authors can decide on it before the registration. Prefer adjustments to framing, reporting and writing over design changes.

## Rules

- Work only from the files in this folder. Do not run code.
- Be concrete: point to sections of the protocol where it helps.
- At most about 1,300 words. Use headings and short bullet lists.
