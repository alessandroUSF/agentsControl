# Review of "Judging Their Own Answers", protocol v2.3

## Short answer

The study is not ready to be carried out as written. What is missing is mostly unfinished work, not a flawed design.

The core design is sound:
- the same answer receives four verdicts, from its author and from an unrelated control, blind and in context;
- the prompt is the cluster for inference;
- the claim wording is fixed in advance;
- verdicts that were not delivered are accounted for explicitly;
- the reference labels come from a probability sample.

I would not open another design round. Before the pilot registration, three things need to happen:
1. Complete the items that your own Section 14.2 lists as open.
2. Fix several errors in the text that would otherwise be registered.
3. Make five small changes that are cheap now and impossible later.

## 2. Errors in the text to fix before registering it

1. **Addition 5's sentence after P3 is wrong in one case.** The rule says "If the interval excludes −7 points, add 'which excludes a deficit of seven points.'" An interval of [−14, −9] excludes −7, yet it shows a deficit larger than seven points.
   - Key the sentence to the lower limit being above −7.
   - Add a neutral third form for an interval that lies entirely below −7.
   - The summary repeats the same rule.
2. **The summary has drifted from the protocol.**
   - The summary gives the cut savings as US$1.63, 4.72 and 2.39. The protocol and the manifest give US$1.61, 4.67 and 2.34.
   - The summary merges "not estimable" and "estimated without an interval" into one ι_W sentence ("could not be estimated"). Round 2 deliberately separated these two cases.
   - The summary should be generated by script for its numbers, as the protocol already is.
3. **Context fit is stated as fact in some places and as an assumption in others.**
   - Section 10.2 states that every primary answer "fits" every local judge, and Section 13.3 rule 3 says something similar.
   - Section 11.3 says that the 25% tokenizer allowance is an assumption.
   - Align the wording.
4. **Minor:**
   - Action 17's machine-time estimate leaves out the crossed subset (14–43 h).
   - The Holm-adjusted p-value for the case where one test is not carried out (presumably 2p) is not defined.

## 3. Changes I would make before the pilot registration

1. **Measure how faithful the reconstruction is, inside the pending rendering check.** For each local model, compare two token sequences:
   - the respondent call: the prompt plus the generated tokens;
   - the in-context judge rendering, up to the end of the assistant turn.

   Report every difference. Examples are the empty think block that a Qwen3-family respondent may have generated before its answer, the `/no_think` switch, and the doubled system prompt of model 6. This puts a number on "reconstructed" for the manipulation the whole study rests on. It uses harmless sentinel texts and costs nothing.
2. **Measure context fit where it is tight.** The pilot never shows an answer of model 22 or 23 to a local judge. Those answers run up to 4,000 tokens in a different tokenizer.
   - Model 21 (Mistral-7B vocabulary) is the worst case.
   - An overflow in the registered arrays scores 0, and an overflow can differ between the two presentations.
   - Add a step that renders and counts tokens, producing no verdict: offline tokenization, or a 1-token call. Run it on every pilot answer of models 22 and 23, and on the longest local pilot answers, under every local judge in both presentations.
3. **The cap of 20 unknown labels in the detection gate.** Section 9.3 already predicts that ordinary skipping (about 22 of the 215 answers in scope) will exceed it. That makes Q4's main statement unassessable for a computational reason, not a statistical one.
   - Keep exact enumeration up to the cap.
   - Above the cap, use any conservative upper bound on the largest upper limit, for example the exact maximum of the estimate plus an upper bound on its standard error. If the conservative bound passes, the statement passes.
4. **The training material will barely exercise Part A.** The material is 24 answers to harmful prompts from models 4–6, and they are probably mostly refusals. Part A closes on this training.
   - Draw round 2, or part of both rounds, from round-1 pilot answers of the refusal-removed models.
   - These answers exist before the pilot's judging calls, lie outside the frames, and are already covered by the second statement in Section 19.3.
5. **Criterion (c4) has no default for unknown authorization.** Criterion (b) does. The examples imply a default ("an explicit story about a named real actor" = yes), but the rule itself does not state it. Add one sentence parallel to (b) now, so the shared instrument is unambiguous for both judges and labellers.

## 4. Points I would weigh but not block on

- **P2's identification.** Appendix A.2 shows that with no author effect, judge heterogeneity aligned with content makes P2 reject 38–45% of the time, while ι_W confirms the rejection only 6–11% of the time.
  - The crossed comparison is the best-identified quantity in the study. Keeping it secondary is defensible, given the claim wording.
  - A cheap improvement with no new calls: register a sensitivity version of the crossed model with judge × author-kind fixed effects (instruction-tuned, abliterated, Dolphin line, Cydonia line). This effect is identified alongside the own-answer term, and it targets the alternative that Section 18.1 names: a judge reacting to its own kind of content.
- **D20.** I would adopt the proposal: keep the t tests and report the fragility count as description only. The counts of prompts with positive and negative totals, already printed, show whether the data are in the sparse regime.
- **P3.**
  - The margin was chosen from the test's precision, so the test is nearly certain to reject.
  - β_S also mixes in severity differences between different judges.
  - The abstract sentence will read as reassurance. Consider leading the sentence with the interval.
- **The pilot does estimate one component of the effect.** Section 13.1 says the pilot estimates no effect. But the pilot reports statuses by presentation, and those are the delivery component of θ_S (A.2: delivery failures alone make P1 reject 87–100% of the time).
  - Rule 1 is symmetric and mechanical, so keep it.
  - Say this in Section 13.1.
