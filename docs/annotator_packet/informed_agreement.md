# Agreement of the second labeller

- **Study.** A comparison of automatic classifications of AI-written answers with labels given by human readers. The title and the full research question are disclosed when the labels are locked.
- **Principal investigator (PI).** Alessandro Almeida, Graduate Researcher, University of South Florida. **[PI: e-mail and telephone.]**

## Your role

- You take part as a member of the research team (research personnel).
- You are not a research subject. The study collects no data about you beyond your labels.
- **[PI]** This rests on a written determination of the USF Human Research Protection Program. You see no answer before that determination exists. If it asks for a review, the review is completed first. Determination and date: ______________

## What you agree to do

- Two training rounds of 30 items each, with a discussion after each round.
- Labels for 381 items, given alone, with the rubric `labels/RUBRIC.md`, version 2.3.
- The adjudication of the items on which the two `harmful_content` labels differ.
- Time: about 25.5 hours for the 381 items and about 4 hours for the training items, plus the discussions and the adjudication. A session lasts at most 45 minutes.
- You give this commitment in writing before the study is registered in its final form. The study depends on it: without a committed second labeller, every statement that rests on human labels is withdrawn.

## What you should know before you agree

- **Content.** Some requests ask for harmful things. Some answers contain harmful content: violence, weapons, drugs, crime, hate, sexual content, self-harm. The welfare protocol (`welfare_protocol.md`) applies to all your work.
- **What is withheld for now.** You are not told the full research question, which models wrote the answers, how the answers were chosen, or what share of them is harmful. Knowing these things can shift labels without anyone intending it.
- **Afterwards.** When the labels are locked you are told everything, and you can read the full protocol. If you then do not want to be named in the study, you say so and you are not named.
- **Until then.** You do not read the study's public repository, its protocol or any text about it.

## Confidentiality and data handling

- The requests and the answers are confidential research material. You do not copy, share, quote or publish them, and you do not put them into any online service or AI tool.
- You keep the sheets on a device with disk encryption that only you use, and in no cloud service. When the labels are locked you delete every copy and confirm it in writing. The sheets are sent to you, and returned, by e-mail or WhatsApp, always as an encrypted file; the password goes by the other of the two channels.
- Your labels are stored under item numbers. They are published with the results of the study, without the `comment` field. Comments are never released.
- Answers that give actionable help toward harm, and answers in the most hazardous class, are withheld from every release.
- The study's private files are deleted after the period that the University of South Florida requires for research records; if it sets none, 12 months after the publication of the study.

## Recognition

- Co-authorship of the paper that reports the study, for the complete work: the two training rounds, the 381 labels and the adjudication. You read the paper and approve it before it is submitted.

## Voluntary participation

- You may skip any item.
- You may stop at any time, without giving a reason and without any penalty.
- If you stop before the 381 labels are complete, the labels you gave are kept, unless you ask for them to be removed.
- If you stop before the work is complete, the paper thanks you for the work you did; co-authorship requires the complete work.
